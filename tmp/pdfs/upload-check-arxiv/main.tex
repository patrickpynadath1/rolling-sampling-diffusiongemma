\begin{abstract}
A diffusion language sampler makes two choices for every position in its denoising window: where the next token comes from, and when the position is committed to the KV cache.
These choices give rise to two extremes: committing tokens on a per-position basis, or committing them at a block level.
Using the entropy-bound sampler in DiffusionGemma, we study the tradeoffs between the two.
We simulate per-position commitment by freezing each token upon acceptance, removing the chance for later revision: macro accuracy falls from 73\% to 20\% as the entropy bound is relaxed, while the unfrozen variants stay near 70\%.
Block-level commitment results in redundant computation, as tokens converge in a largely left-to-right order and, on average, more than half of the slots are spent on already-final tokens by twenty denoising steps.
We introduce the Rolling Entropy Sampler (RES), which waits for a sufficiently long contiguous accepted prefix, allowing accepted positions farther ahead to remain available for revision without waiting for the entire window.
Across seven benchmarks, RES raises tokens per forward pass by 32.5\% at an average cost of 1.9 points of accuracy, and extends the speed-quality frontier beyond what relaxing the entropy bound alone can reach.
\end{abstract}
