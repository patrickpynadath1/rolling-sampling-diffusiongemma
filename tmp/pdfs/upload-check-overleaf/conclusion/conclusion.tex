We studied two choices made by diffusion language samplers: where the next token comes from, and when the position is committed to the KV cache.
Using the EB sampler in DiffusionGemma, we examined per-position commitment and block-level commitment. Freezing tokens upon acceptance simulates the loss of later revision under per-position commitment, which degrades performance at higher entropy bounds. Block-level commitment instead tolerates redundant computation on positions that have already converged.
RES strikes a balance by waiting for a sufficiently long contiguous accepted prefix. Accepted positions farther ahead retain the chance for revision, while the prefix can leave without waiting for the entire window.
Across seven benchmarks, RES raises tokens per forward pass by 32.5\% at an average cost of 1.9 points of accuracy and extends the frontier at high throughput. Instruction following degrades more than other tasks, and the block sampler remains better at low throughput.
