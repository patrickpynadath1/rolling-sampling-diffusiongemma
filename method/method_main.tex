Here we introduce the Rolling Entropy Sampler (RES), a middle ground between per-position commitment and block-level commitment. Rather than committing each token as soon as it is accepted or waiting for the entire window, RES waits until a contiguous prefix of at least $\ell_{\min}$ positions has been accepted by the EB rule.
The core mechanism is simple: instead of waiting for the entire block to converge, RES encodes any sufficiently long contiguous prefix of accepted tokens, appends it to the KV cache, and refills the freed slots with fresh noise.
An accepted position farther ahead remains free to change until the preceding positions are also accepted and the minimum-shift condition is met. Waiting for the contiguous prefix preserves the chance for later revision without retaining the entire denoising window.

\paragraph{Commitment Rule}
In the terms of Section~\ref{sec:motivation}, a rolling sampler is an adaptive sampler with a non-trivial commitment rule.
RES repurposes the acceptance rule for the EB sampler to also serve as the commitment rule: once a sufficiently large contiguous prefix of positions has been accepted under the EB rule, these positions are appended to the KV cache. 
The entropy bound criterion controls the predictive entropies of jointly accepted positions. Under the low-prediction-error assumption discussed in Section~\ref{sec:prelims}, \eqref{eq:eb-bound} approximately bounds the joint dependence error incurred by factorized sampling \citep{ben2026accelerated}.
Thus we use the EB criterion to define $F_{commit}$ as well as $F_{route}$: RES commits the longest prefix of the window whose positions all lie in the accepted set $A_{\gamma}$, provided it meets the minimum shift criterion below (Table~\ref{tab:samplers}).
\begin{align*}
    F_{commit}(X)_i &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\, i \le \ell(A_\gamma) \,\right] \, \mathbb{1}\!\left[\, \ell(A_\gamma) \ge \ell_{\min} \,\right] \\
    A_\gamma &= \arg\max_{U} |U| \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
    \sum_{i \in U} H\big(P_\theta^i(\cdot \mid X_t)\big) - \max_{i \in U} H\big(P_\theta^i(\cdot \mid X_t)\big) \le \gamma \\
    \ell(A) &= \max \{\, j : \{1, \dots, j\} \subseteq A \,\}
\end{align*}
Here, $A_{\gamma}$ represents the set of accepted positions under the EB rule, given entropy threshold $\gamma$; and $\ell(A)$ is the largest contiguous prefix of accepted positions in $A_{\gamma}$. 
We discuss the minimum shift criterion below. 

\paragraph{Minimum Shift Criterion}
To interpolate smoothly between Unified EB and fully incremental commitment, we introduce a minimum shift criterion: the contiguous prefix must contain at least $\ell_{\min}$ tokens in order to be appended to the KV cache. This gives another axis for trading off performance for speed: by decreasing $\ell_{\min}$, it is possible to accelerate RES without altering the entropy bound.
The minimum shift requires acceptance across a sufficiently long prefix before commitment, while leaving accepted positions farther ahead available for revision.

RES is distinct from doing block diffusion with a smaller block size: while block diffusion denoises and commits each block before moving to the next, RES commits a prefix while retaining the remaining positions in the denoising window.
This can be seen as *warm starting* the positions ahead of the committed prefix, as these positions preserve their denoising progress across shifts and remain available for further revision.


\subsection{Additional Modifications}
RES includes two further design choices, and we also note a property that follows from them. 

First, we use a position-based temperature schedule. Positions toward the left of the window have been denoised for longer than those that have just entered on the right, and the schedule reflects this, thus improving speed. 

Second, we merge the encoding and denoising steps. RES commits short prefixes frequently, so a separate encoding pass for each commit would cost more forward passes than rolling saves; merging makes rolling commitment efficient. All reported TPF numbers count every forward pass for both samplers, including Unified EB’s per-block encoding pass. 

Finally, because the temperature schedule no longer depends on the maximum number of denoising steps, that maximum acts only as a budget. We do not evaluate this property here.
\paragraph{Position-based Temperature Schedule}
One natural consequence of the autoregressive bias of DiffusionGemma is that a token's position in the rolling canvas provides a proxy for how close it is to having converged.
Thus we can modify the standard temperature shaping applied in DiffusionGemma to be based on position rather than noise level.
This accelerates tokens that are close to the prefix, as these are closer to being committed, while allowing for positions in the suffix to explore candidate tokens.

\paragraph{Simultaneous Encoding and Denoising}
One other advantage of RES is that the number of tokens appended to the KV cache is much smaller than the total canvas. This allows for merging both the encoding step and the denoising step present in DiffusionGemma.
More specifically, given a window of $w$ positions and a contiguous prefix of $\ell$ accepted tokens, we use a causal mask for positions $0$ to $\ell-1$, while positions $\ell$ to $w-1$ have bidirectional attention.
This keeps the number of processed positions per forward pass the same between the original DiffusionGemma sampler and RES, while ensuring that every step is performing denoising.

\paragraph{Anytime Sampling}
While Unified EB is highly adaptive, the temperature schedule is tied to the maximum DNS, which is a hyper-parameter that must be set.
Ideally, the maximum DNS would only serve a time-out function to prevent sampler stalls, as opposed to controlling the logit shaping.
One consequence of adopting the position-based temperature schedule is that the logit shaping becomes independent of the maximum number of DNS, making it an \textit{anytime sampler}: the maximum number of denoising steps serves as a budget limit rather than controlling fundamental sampler behavior.
