\begin{abstract}
A diffusion language sampler makes two choices for every position in its denoising window: where the next token comes from, and when the position is committed to the KV cache.
Using the entropy-bound sampler in DiffusionGemma, we study two extremes of the second choice.
Committing each token as soon as it is accepted removes the chance to revise it, which hurts when the entropy bound is relaxed to trade accuracy for speed.
When accepted tokens are frozen, macro accuracy falls from 73\% to 20\% as the bound is relaxed, while the unfrozen sampler stays near 70\%.
Committing a whole block at once wastes computation, since tokens converge in a largely left-to-right order and more than half of the window holds already-final tokens by twenty denoising steps.
These findings motivate the Rolling Entropy Sampler (RES), which commits a contiguous accepted prefix once it reaches a minimum length and keeps positions farther ahead open to revision.
Unlike earlier rolling samplers, RES runs on uniform diffusion, where accepted tokens can still change, and it needs no training.
Across seven benchmarks, RES increases tokens per forward pass from 19.3 to 25.5, while macro accuracy goes from 80.7\% to 78.8\%, and it extends the speed-quality Pareto frontier beyond what tuning the entropy bound alone reaches.
\end{abstract}
